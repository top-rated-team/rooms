Most “lead quality problems” in Google Ads don’t come from keywords or match types.

They come from the absence of intent signals the algorithm can actually see and learn from.

In 2026, if we don’t feed Google a clean, fast intent outcome, it will optimize for the wrong thing: cheap forms, wrong buyers, stale timelines. The fix isn’t another negative keyword sweep. It’s a better signal.

Here’s the fastest way we’ve found to create one in 30 days: use LinkedIn Lead Gen Forms as a signal factory, then mirror the winning questions into your website forms and import those qualified outcomes back into Google Ads as value‑based conversions.

## Why LinkedIn first?

- We can ask buyers direct, high‑intent questions inside the form. No dev sprint. No CMS drama.
- We get fast readouts on what answers correlate with meetings and proposals.
- We can iterate the question set weekly without breaking site UX.

Once we know which questions and answers separate buyers from browsers, we copy them into our website forms. Now every Google click can be graded instantly. That grade becomes the conversion Google trains on.

## The Self‑Declared Intent Loop (SDI Loop)

1) Define the qualifying questions

Pick 3–5 fields that consistently predict sales readiness. Make them multiple‑choice to speed completion and scoring.

Good starters:
- Timeframe: “When are you planning to start?” (0–30 / 31–90 / 90+ days)
- Budget band: aligned to your price points (e.g., <$5k, $5–20k, $20k+)
- Role in purchase: Decision maker / Influencer / Researcher
- Primary use case: choose your ICP‑specific categories
- Tech context: “Which tools do you currently use?”

Avoid free text here. Save open questions for later.

2) Build the scoring rubric

Keep it simple. We want a score that sorts into three buckets our sales team trusts.

Example weighting:
- Timeline 0–30 days = +40; 31–90 = +25; 90+ = +5
- Budget top tier = +40; mid = +20; low = +5
- Decision maker = +30; Influencer = +10; Researcher = 0
- ICP use case = +20; adjacent = +10; non‑ICP = −20
- Disqualifiers (student, vendor, competitor) = −50

Buckets:
- 70+ = Sales‑Ready (SR)
- 40–69 = Marketing‑Qualified (MQ)
- <40 = Disqualify (DQ)

3) Test on LinkedIn Lead Gen Forms

- Run a tight audience against your ICP (titles, functions, industries, company sizes).
- Use a clear, specific offer (demo, pricing walkthrough, compliance checklist). Vague offers attract vague buyers.
- Collect 50–150 form fills to see patterns. You’re not trying to scale yet. You’re trying to learn which answers predict booked calls.

4) Validate with downstream outcomes

- Track booked meetings and show rates by answer choice. In many accounts, timeline and role are the fastest “tells.”
- Confirm with sales that SR bucket feels right. Adjust weights if needed. Two rounds usually get you there.

5) Mirror the winning fields on your website forms

- Add the same multiple‑choice fields (same labels and options) to your site forms used by all traffic, including Google Ads.
- Keep the form tight. We aim for 6–8 total fields, with the new qualifiers replacing low‑value fields (e.g., generic “Company size” if it never predicts anything).

6) Turn scores into Google‑learnable conversions

- Auto‑score submissions in your CRM/automation (simple if/else rules are enough).
- Create a “Qualified Lead” conversion in Google Ads tied to the SR and MQ buckets only. Exclude DQ.
- Assign values by bucket (e.g., SR = 100, MQ = 30) rather than guessing LTV. Step values are easier for bidding and less brittle.
- Import these as offline conversions with GCLID/GBRAID or via enhanced conversions for leads. Aim for imports within 7 days of the click.

7) Switch bidding to value

- Move key Search campaigns to “Maximize conversion value” with a sensible target once you have stable signal volume.
- For top‑of‑funnel terms, keep a CPA goal on the qualified event if value volume is thin. The rule is simple: bid only on what you’re willing to buy repeatedly.

## What changes once Google sees real intent

- Your RSA headlines and sitelinks start winning the right auctions because the system learns which queries produce SR/MQ outcomes.
- Low‑intent searches that used to “look cheap” but never closed lose budget naturally.
- You stop arguing about “lead quality” because the feedback loop is explicit: buyers tell you their timeline, role, and budget—up front.

## 30‑Day Sprint Plan

Week 1 — Design and launch
- Draft 3–5 qualifying questions.
- Launch LinkedIn Lead Gen test with a specific offer.
- Set up scoring and bucket logic. Prep CRM fields.

Week 2 — Pattern read
- Hit 50–100 forms. Meet with sales twice.
- Tune weights. Kill questions no one answers well. Add one if a blind spot appears.

Week 3 — Mirror and import
- Add identical fields to website forms.
- Create Qualified Lead conversion(s) in Google Ads.
- Start offline conversion imports with values.

Week 4 — Bid and scale
- Shift core Search to value bidding on the Qualified event.
- Pause terms/ad groups that don’t produce SR/MQ after 300–500 clicks.
- Brief new ad copy using exact buyer phrasing from the winning use‑case answers.

## Playbooks that compound the gains

- Negative keywords from disqualifiers: If “student” or “DIY” shows up in DQ reasons, add those to negatives and ad copy filters.
- Audience signals: Build custom segments from winning job titles and use‑case phrases. Pair them with exact/match‑type intent to stabilize learnings.
- Asset mapping: Create RSA variants for each use case surfaced in the forms. Message wins auctions. Generic loses.
- Sales ops sync: Route SR to fast‑track calendars. MQ to nurture. Never let SR wait.

## Pitfalls to avoid

- Asking too much, too soon. Three strong qualifiers beat nine soft ones.
- Free‑text answers. They stall scoring and tank form completion.
- Import delays. If your Qualified event lands in Google Ads two weeks late, bidding won’t move. Keep it under 7 days.
- Value fiction. Don’t assign fantasy LTV. Step values tied to buckets are safer and teach the model faster.
- Dirty UTMs. Make sure GCLID is captured on your site forms and carried into the CRM. Dedupe by click ID to avoid double imports.

## What if LinkedIn volume is low?

- Tighten creative. Call out the ICP in the headline. Name the job function. Name the problem.
- Broaden industry or title slightly, but keep the qualifier questions sharp. The form is your filter.
- Run a small retargeting set to capture known site visitors with the same form. The goal is enough data to validate the rubric, not to max out spend on LinkedIn.

## Why this works

- Algorithms don’t guess intent. They react to what we feed them.
- Self‑declared answers beat inferred signals when budgets are tight and timelines matter.
- The loop is cross‑platform: LinkedIn is where we learn which questions separate buyers; Google is where we scale by bidding only on qualified outcomes.

## The takeaway

If we keep training Google on raw form fills, we’ll keep buying conversations our sales team can’t win.

Build the SDI Loop. Ask sharper questions on LinkedIn, mirror them on your website, and import Qualified outcomes into Google Ads with values. Give the system a signal that matches how your revenue team actually makes money.
